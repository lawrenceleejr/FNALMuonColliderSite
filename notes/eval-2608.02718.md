# Evaluating the corridor neutrino studies against arXiv:2608.02718

**Reference.** J.-Y. Choi, M. Hostert, P. Li, Z. Liu, *The Forward Neutrino Flux
and its Secondaries at a 10 TeV Muon Collider*, [arXiv:2608.02718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02718)
(UMN-TH-4432/25, 3 Aug 2026). 27 pp. Introduces **MINT**, a Monte Carlo that
decays muons along a real MAD-X lattice — the IMCC hybrid v0.6 + v0.9 interaction
region — and propagates the neutrinos to a downstream detector plane.

This is the first published simulation of the *same physical object* this study
is built on: the forward neutrino beam from the IP straight of a 10 TeV muon
collider. It is directly comparable and it overturns one load-bearing assumption.

---

## 1. Bottom line

One paper result matters more than all the others:

> "Because the muon beam needs to be strongly focused at the interaction point,
> the resulting neutrino flux spot size is dominated by the muon beam divergence
> rather than the decay kinematics, θ<sub>μ</sub> ∼ O(0.1 mrad) ≫ 1/γ ∼ 0.02 mrad,
> washing out neutrino energy-angle correlations (the so-called prism effect)."
> — §V, Conclusions

The corridor study's beam-size page asserts the opposite: that the far-field core
is kinematic, **r = L/γ**, with beam divergence confined to a 1.7 %-weight halo
from the ±L\* ≈ ±6 m final-focus drift. That is wrong for the lattice the IMCC
actually has, and the error is ~10× in radius / ~10² in on-axis flux density.

The consequences split cleanly:

| | verdict |
|---|---|
| Siting geometry, ±500 ft envelope walk, stratigraphy, exit locations | **unaffected** — centreline geometry, not beam width |
| Radiation safety (Zone A–D doses, UIUC scenario) | **survives, on better footing** — see §4 |
| Aquifer audit | **survives with ~100× more margin** — see §4 |
| Total-flux claims (ν/yr crossing a plane, Ci/yr of tritium, ν<sub>τ</sub>/yr delivered) | **unaffected** — see §4 |
| ν<sub>τ</sub> signal-to-background *ratios*, baseline-independence of the per-tonne rate | **unaffected** — flux-normalisation-independent |
| Spot sizes, per-tonne interaction rates, the one-tonne detector, exit-strip widths, the prism/energy-angle table, the absolute ν<sub>τ</sub> per-tonne rate | **must be revised** — see §3 |
| Rock-muon backgrounds, rock-produced ν<sub>τ</sub>, chicane/arc flux plateaus | **missing entirely** — see §5 |

The study's central *siting* argument is intact. Its central *detector* argument
is not, unless the machine is given a beam line it does not currently have (§6).

## 2. What the paper establishes

Machine assumptions, for reference against ours:

| | paper | this study |
|---|---|---|
| E<sub>μ</sub> / γ | 5 TeV / 47 300 | same |
| μ per bunch per beam | 2 × 10¹² | 1.8 × 10¹² |
| Injection / live time | 5 Hz, 10⁷ s/yr | 5 Hz, 1.2 × 10⁷ s/yr |
| Decays reaching the ring | 1.0 × 10²⁰/yr/beam, 85 % decay | 0.97 × 10²⁰/yr/sign |
| Circumference | 10 km | 11 km |
| IP straight | **±180 m (3.6 % of ring)** | **700 m (6.4 %)** |
| ε<sub>N</sub> | 25 µm·rad | 25 µm·rad (same) |
| β\* | 1.5 mm | 1.5 mm (same) |
| Forward ν/yr | 1.2 × 10¹⁹ (both beams, both flavours) | 1.24 × 10¹⁹ (one direction, one sign) |

So the normalisations agree; the *only* substantive difference is that our
straight is 1.94× longer, and that we assume it is collinear and it is not.

### 2.1 The divergence, from the real lattice

Appendix B is the passage that settles it:

* At the IP, β\* = 1.5 mm gives σ\*<sub>θ</sub> = √(ε<sub>N</sub>/γβ\*) ≈ **0.6 mrad** —
  28 × the 1/γ = 0.021 mrad decay cone. Our beam-size page has this number right.
* "*The straight section, however, is much longer than the drift section between
  the final focusing magnets. In the current design, the angular divergence for
  the longest drift section, ∼150 m, is about 0.1 − 0.2 mrad, which is the more
  appropriate scale for the angular divergence of the neutrino beam.*"
* "*The interaction region is designed to be dispersion-free… In the matching
  sections, however, the dispersive contribution can dominate the local angular
  spread.*"

Two of our claims fail against this. First, the drift between the final-focus
magnets is **~150 m long, not ±6 m** — and a drift conserves angles, so all
150 m of it radiates at 0.1–0.2 mrad. Second, our assertion that "beyond the
quads the beam is recollimated: in the matching sections the divergence is
µrad-scale, far below 1/γ" is contradicted directly: in the matching sections
*dispersion* sets the angular spread, and it is larger, not smaller.

*(Coincidence worth noting: `L_PENCIL = 150 m` in `tools/corridor_layout.py` is
exactly the length of the paper's longest drift. We guessed the right length and
gave it the wrong divergence.)*

### 2.2 Cross-check: the effective spot

The paper quotes an on-axis flux of **2 × 10¹⁴ ν/cm²/yr at 5 km** (both flavours,
one beam), from 2 × 3.06 × 10¹⁸ = 6.1 × 10¹⁸ forward neutrinos. A Gaussian that
peaks there has

  σ = √(N / 2πΦ<sub>0</sub>) = 70 cm at 5 km → **σ<sub>θ</sub> = 0.14 mrad = 6.6 × (1/γ)**

and the implied on-axis density suppression relative to the pure-kinematic
formula Φ = Nγ²/πL² is 2γ²σ<sub>θ</sub>² = **87×**. Computing Nγ²/πL² directly for
the paper's own straight gives 1.75 × 10¹⁶ ν/cm²/yr — 87 × their simulated value.
Two independent routes, same answer. The paper's second anchor agrees: 60 % of
the flux inside r = 1.3 m at 5 km, i.e. 0.26 mrad, which a boosted-decay profile
convolved with σ<sub>θ</sub> = 0.15–0.20 mrad reproduces.

**Take σ<sub>θ</sub> ≈ 0.15 mrad (7 × 1/γ) and an on-axis density suppression of
~10² as the numbers to design against.**

### 2.3 Two features we have no counterpart for

Fig. 3 and Fig. 20 show, beyond the smeared core:

* a **chicane plateau** out to θ ≲ 3 mrad — the chicanes that sweep beam
  electrons/positrons onto the walls tilt the local orbit tangent;
* a **broader arc plateau** at larger angles.

Together these carry O(1 %) of the forward flux at ~10⁻⁴ of the core surface
density. At 197 km, 3 mrad is a **590 m radius** — so the far exit has a
kilometre-scale faint halo around the core, not just the core.

## 3. What must be revised

### 3.1 Spot sizes (`content/beamsize.md` §1–§3, `static/tool/`)

Boosted-decay profile convolved with σ<sub>θ</sub> = 0.15 mrad:

| location | L | published r₅₀ | **revised r₅₀** | published r₉₉ | **revised r₉₉** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| detector hall | 1.30 km | 2.7 cm | **24 cm** | 27 cm | **63 cm** |
| north beam leaves ground | 2.18 km | 4.6 cm | **39 cm** | 46 cm | **1.05 m** |
| Fermilab fence | 2.84 km | 6.0 cm | **51 cm** | 60 cm | **1.4 m** |
| Smith Road | 10.1 km | 21 cm | **1.8 m** | 2.1 m | **4.9 m** |
| under Urbana | 193 km | 4.1 m | **35 m** | 41 m | **93 m** |
| UIUC South Farms exit | 197.4 km | 4.2 m | **36 m** | 42 m | **95 m** |

The universal-profile machinery in §1 is still right *as the decay kernel*; it is
no longer the answer, because the kernel is now narrower than the smearing.
The containment table (50/90/99 % at 0.64/1.47/3.00 L/γ for interactions) rests
on the energy-angle correlation and **must go** — see §3.4.

### 3.2 The one-tonne detector (`content/beamsize.md` §4) — the biggest casualty

At 1.3 km, a ⌀16 cm cylinder subtends 62 µrad:

| detector radius at 1.3 km | fraction of flux, published model | **fraction, revised** |
|---|---|---|
| 8 cm (the 1 t tungsten cylinder) | 89 % | **7.8 %** |
| 20 cm | 98 % | 40 % |
| 50 cm | 99.7 % | 95 % |
| **63 cm** (99 % containment) | — | **99 %** |

A 2.4 m tungsten cylinder of 63 cm radius weighs **57 t**, not 1 t. The claim
that "the same physics reach comes from a target small enough to sit inside a
spectrometer… an object you could carry on a truck" does not survive: the beam
is no longer smaller than the detector.

Rate in the *published* 1 t cylinder (⌀16 cm × 2.4 m, ρℓ = 4.6 × 10³ g/cm²,
CSMS σ summed over the two species): **1.2 × 10¹² /yr → 1.1 × 10¹¹ /yr**, an 11×
cut. Note this is much less than 100× because tungsten's column density is so
large that intercepting 8 % of the beam still yields a lot — the sharper loss is
in *flux density*, not total rate.

Worth adopting instead: the paper's detector philosophy is the same idea done
right for a 1 m beam — "trade target mass for improved particle identification"
— a 3.2 t **low-density gaseous-argon TPC**, 39 m long, ρℓ = 43 g/cm², inside a
1.3 m → 2.4 m conical aperture, with a vertex tracker sized to the *centre* of
the beam and a dipole for TeV charge ID. That is a better match to our on-site
hall than a tungsten slug, and it is a design we can cite rather than invent.

### 3.3 Per-tonne rates and flux densities

Every on-axis flux density and per-tonne rate in the study divides by ~10²:

| quantity | published | **revised** |
|---|---|---|
| on-axis flux at the detector (1.3 km, per species) | 2.6 × 10¹⁷ ν/cm²/yr | **2.6 × 10¹⁵** |
| interactions per tonne-year at 1.3 km | 7 × 10¹² | **6 × 10¹⁰** |
| on-axis flux at the UIUC exit (per species) | 1.1 × 10¹³ ν/cm²/yr | **1.1 × 10¹¹** |
| interactions per tonne-year at the exit | 2.7 × 10⁸ | **2.7 × 10⁶** |

"Some eight orders of magnitude beyond FASERν" (`design.md`) becomes ~six. Still
the most intense TeV neutrino exposure ever contemplated — the paper makes the
same claim for the same beam — but the margin is smaller than we state.

### 3.4 The prism effect is gone

`beamsize.md` §1 and §5 both lean on ⟨E⟩(θ) = ⟨E⟩₀/(1 + γ²θ²):

* §1's tighter interaction profile ((1+x²)⁻³ rather than (1+x²)⁻²) — **delete**;
* §5's note 2, "the oscillated flux is not a pencil… exactly cancelling the
  beamed flux profile: the ν<sub>τ</sub> surface density is nearly flat in solid
  angle" — **delete**. With E decorrelated from θ, P(ν<sub>μ</sub>→ν<sub>τ</sub>)
  no longer grows off-axis, so the ν<sub>τ</sub> density simply follows the flux
  profile. The *conclusion* (per-tonne rate insensitive to detector radius)
  weakens to "insensitive within the core".

Fig. 7 of the paper is the direct demonstration: the clean radius–energy prism
without beam dynamics, washed into a broad blob with it.

### 3.5 ν<sub>τ</sub> appearance (`beamsize.md` §5, `safety.md` §4)

Flux-independent claims **survive unchanged**: the L²/L² cancellation that makes
the per-tonne rate baseline-independent, the S/B ratios (1.6 × 10⁻¹² near,
3.8 × 10⁻⁸ far), the 23 000× far-site advantage, the eV²-scale sterile lever arm.
The absolute rate does not:

| | published | **revised** |
|---|---|---|
| ν<sub>τ</sub> CC per tonne-year (any L, on axis) | 6.6 | **~0.07** |
| 100 t far detector on South Farms | 660 ν<sub>τ</sub> CC/yr | **~7/yr** |
| oscillation-made ν<sub>τ</sub> crossing the exit plane | 4 × 10¹¹/yr | ~2 × 10¹¹/yr (total flux — see below) |

The last row is a *total-flux* number and so largely survives; it drops only by
~2× because the published value used the off-axis softening (P ∝ 1/E²) that §3.4
removes. "~8 × 10¹¹ oscillation-made ν<sub>τ</sub> per year delivered to ground
level" becomes ~4 × 10¹¹ — still the headline it was meant to be.

But "a 100-tonne far detector would make ~660 ν<sub>τ</sub> CC/yr, two orders of
magnitude beyond the world sample" becomes ~7/yr — DONUT-and-OPERA-per-year, not
100× the world sample. Combined with the τ-ID caveat the page already states
honestly, the far-site ν<sub>τ</sub> case gets materially harder.

### 3.6 Exit strips (`safety.md` §3–§4, `beamsize.md` §3)

| exit | contour | published | **revised** |
|---|---|---|---|
| north (on site, 2.18 km, 15.7 mrad) | 50 % | 9 cm × 5.9 m | **0.8 m × 50 m** |
| | 99 % | 0.9 m × 60 m | **2.1 m × 130 m** |
| UIUC South Farms (197 km, 15.6 mrad) | 50 % | 8.3 m × 534 m | **71 m × 4.6 km** |
| | 99 % | 83 m × 5.3 km | **190 m × 12 km** |

"The surface-grazing exit is, at this range, a patch of lawn" holds for the north
exit — it is a wide parking space instead of a narrow one, still 655 m inside the
fence. The southern strip is the problem: the *unsegmented* 50 % core is now
71 m × 4.6 km ≈ 0.33 km², where `safety.md` §4 describes "an ~8 m-wide core".
The land action is still one-institution scale on UIUC research farmland, but it
is a *quarter-section* of farmland, not a fenced sidewalk, and the page's framing
should say so. (The page's own segmented figure — "stretches toward ~12 km along
the meridian at proportionally lower dose" — is close to the revised 99 % strip;
the difference is that this width is now unavoidable rather than a chosen trade.)

### 3.7 A bug in the interactive tool

`static/tool/index.html` around line 970:

```js
// Muon-beam optics: geometric emittance eps_N/gamma, betatron function
// beta(s) = beta* + s^2/beta*, angular divergence sqrt(eps_g/beta(s)).
// The divergence is LARGEST at the IP (smallest beta) and falls away fast.
```

The comment's last sentence is wrong, and `beamsize.md` §2 already says why:
in a field-free drift the Courant–Snyder invariant γ<sub>tw</sub> = (1+α²)/β is
constant, so σ<sub>θ</sub> = √(εγ<sub>tw</sub>) = √(ε/β\*) **everywhere in the
drift** — it does not fall off as √(ε/β(s)). The tool's `sCross` (the β where
divergence equals 1/γ) evaluates to β = εγ² = 1.18 m, hence s ≈ 4.2 cm and
`haloFrac` ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ — the "1e-4-weight halo" in the line-988 comment. The
prose page was corrected to the ±L\* picture (1.7 %); the tool was not, so the
two disagree by 140×, and both are now superseded.

The tool's β\* slider is also mis-advertised. §2 of the page says the core radii
"do not move" over β\* = 0.1–20 mm. That is only true because the model gives the
divergence ~10⁻⁴ weight. Once the divergence sets the core, β\* — or rather the
β of the section that actually radiates down the corridor — becomes *the*
parameter of the whole study, and the slider should show the core moving.

## 4. What survives — and what is strengthened

### 4.1 Radiation safety: the same answers, better justified

`safety.md` reaches its numbers through two knobs it flags as speculative: an
"effective collinear pencil length" of 150 m out of 700 m (a 4.7× credit, taken
by *discarding* the other 550 m of decays), and a hypothesised ±0.5 mrad vertical
segmentation of the insertion (a 15–25× peak dilution). Replacing both with the
paper's measured plume — all 700 m radiating into σ<sub>θ</sub> = 0.15 mrad, so
the on-axis density falls by 2γ²σ<sub>θ</sub>² = 101 — gives:

| exit | published "mitigated" | published "optimistic" | **paper-based, no segmentation** | **+ ±0.5 mrad segmentation** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| north default, 38.2 km | 26 mSv/yr | 5.2 | **24 mSv/yr** | 5.6 |
| south default, 27.9 km | 66 mSv/yr | 13 | **46 mSv/yr** | 11 |
| **UIUC South Farms, 199 km** | **0.78 mSv/yr** | 0.16 | **0.90 mSv/yr** | **0.21** |

The headline doses land within ~30 % of what the site already publishes — but now
*without* the segmentation assumption and *without* throwing away 79 % of the
decays. The mitigation the study hypothesised is largely delivered for free by
the real optics, and deliberate segmentation becomes an additional ~4× on top.
Every structural conclusion holds: the default exits are unacceptable, the
south side binds, the UIUC scenario lands just under the 1 mSv/yr public limit
and reaches the 0.1 mSv/yr design goal with modest extra mitigation.

This is the most useful thing the paper does for us: it converts the
`L_PENCIL`/`SEG_SPREAD` pair — which `safety.md` correctly identifies as "the
parameter [that] dominates the result and is the first thing a real lattice +
FLUKA study must pin down" — into a quantity taken from a real lattice.

**Caveat to state plainly:** the near-cancellation is a coincidence of two
comparable dilutions, and it is only valid if the corridor beam really is the
IMCC IP straight. Under the dedicated-low-divergence-straight design of §6, the
plume gets *sharper* and the doses go back up toward the raw values. Safety and
detector rate now pull in opposite directions, which they did not before.

### 4.2 Aquifer audit: ~100× more margin

Concentration claims scale with on-axis flux density, so all of them improve:

| claim | published | **revised** |
|---|---|---|
| interactions per litre-year in on-axis water at exit | 9 × 10⁴ | **9 × 10²** |
| saturation ³H in stagnant in-core water at exit | 0.1 Bq/L | **0.001 Bq/L** (vs 740 Bq/L MCL) |
| worst potable-aquifer crossing beyond the fence | ≥ 45× below MCL | **≥ ~4 500× below** |
| range within which any standard could be approached | 2.1 km of the IP | **~210 m of the IP** |
| whole-chord ³H production | 0.06 Ci/yr | **0.06 Ci/yr (unchanged)** |

The headline "the only place any groundwater standard could be approached is a
centimetres-wide filament within 2.1 km of the collision point" becomes a
*decimetres-to-metres*-wide filament within a couple of hundred metres — i.e.
inside the collider hall's own footprint. Total production is a total-flux
quantity and does not move.

### 4.3 Unaffected

Centreline geometry (exit latitudes, depths, grazing angles, chord, dip-aware
stratigraphy), the whole `envelope.md` ±500 ft walk, "one neutrino in 10¹¹
interacts over the 198 km chord", "~2 × 10¹⁵ interactions per year in all of the
rock", the arc-disk and RCS dose zones (those are arcs, where the paper's own
footnote 1 confirms wobbling applies), and every flux-normalisation-independent
ratio.

## 5. Physics the study is missing

### 5.1 Rock muons — the omission that matters for the on-site detector

"About two secondary high-energy and highly polarized muons from the rock would
cross each detector per bunch crossing" — ⟨E<sub>μ</sub>⟩ ≈ 1.2 TeV, O(10¹²)
accepted µ±/yr through a 1.3 m face at 5 km, **40–45× the number of all-flavour
CC+NC neutrino interactions in a 1 kg/cm² detector**, produced mostly in the last
kilometre of rock, and arriving within tens of ps of the neutrinos so that
timing cannot reject them. The paper adds a dedicated muon-halo monitor to the
front of its detector for exactly this.

`beamsize.md` §4 discusses detector sizing with no mention of it. Scaling the
paper's 0.38 µ/m²/BX at 5 km by 1/L² to our 1.3 km hall gives ~5.6 µ/m²/BX;
through the published ⌀16 cm face that is ~0.11 µ per bunch crossing, or
~3 × 10³ µ/s at the 27 kHz crossing rate of an 11 km ring — tolerable, but only
because the detector is small, and it grows as r² for any detector actually
sized to the beam. This belongs in the detector discussion.

### 5.2 Rock-produced ν<sub>τ</sub> — a new irreducible background to the far-site case

The paper computes ν<sub>τ</sub> made by primary neutrinos interacting in the rock
(CC charm → D<sub>(s)</sub> → τν<sub>τ</sub> dominant, plus inverse τ decay,
resonant D\*<sub>s</sub>, and CC ℓτ tridents): 2.0 × 10⁹ ν<sub>τ</sub>+ν̄<sub>τ</sub>
per year through a 1.3 m face at 5 km, giving 0.2 CC events/yr in 3.2 t. It is a
background nobody in this study has considered, and it competes with the *whole*
ν<sub>τ</sub>-appearance argument, because these are genuine ν<sub>τ</sub> — not
mis-identified ν<sub>μ</sub>.

Scaling from the paper's benchmark (secondary ν<sub>τ</sub> density tracks the
*local* primary flux density, since production is dominated by the rock
immediately upstream, and the secondaries spread over ~1 mrad rather than the
beam's 0.15 mrad):

| | rock-produced ν<sub>τ</sub> | oscillation ν<sub>τ</sub> | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| paper's benchmark, 5 km | 3.9 × 10⁴ /cm²/yr | 2.0 × 10³ /cm²/yr | 19 |
| our on-site detector, 1.3 km | ~1.6 × 10⁵ /cm²/yr | 2.0 × 10³ /cm²/yr | **~80** |
| UIUC South Farms, 197 km | ~1.6 × 10² /cm²/yr | ~2.1 × 10³ /cm²/yr | **~0.08** |

Two conclusions, both useful:

1. **The far site survives**, with a ~10 %-level irreducible ν<sub>τ</sub>
   background that is much softer than the beam (⟨E⟩ ~ 300 GeV vs ~3 TeV), so it
   is separable on energy. It must be modelled, not ignored.
2. **The near site's ν<sub>τ</sub> are essentially all rock-made** — outnumbering
   oscillation ν<sub>τ</sub> by ~10². This *sharpens* the page's "the near site
   cannot [attack ν<sub>τ</sub> appearance] at all" from a rate statement into a
   background statement, and incidentally identifies a physics measurement the
   near detector *could* make: charm-induced ν<sub>τ</sub> production in rock.

These are order-of-magnitude scalings off the paper's benchmark, not a
simulation; a MINT run at 197 km would settle them. **Flag them as scalings.**

### 5.3 Also worth folding in

* **Wrong-sign neutrinos** at O(10⁻⁹) of the primary rate — reassuring for the
  "each exit plane is a single-sign beam" claim in `beamsize.md` §5, and worth
  citing as the bound.
* **Polarised TeV muon beam.** The rock-muon flux is ~100× below COMPASS but
  P<sub>μ</sub> ≈ ∓1 and ⟨E<sub>μ</sub>⟩ ≈ 1.2 TeV. For our on-site hall that is
  a second experiment in the same cavern.
* **Ref. [47]** — Burk, Han, Kilian, Kling, Kopp, Tabrizi, on ν<sub>τ</sub> from
  muon and e± collisions with the ring walls — is a further ν<sub>τ</sub>
  background source, noted in the paper's "Note Added" as compatible and
  complementary. Should be checked against §5 of the beam-size page.
* **Timing.** The neutrino pulse is ~5 ps and the µ–ν delay over 360 m of straight
  is 0.3 fs, so neither timing nor transverse position identifies where along the
  straight a neutrino was born. Any future claim about localising decay position
  in our geometry should account for this.

## 6. The design response

The paper's result is about *the IP straight of a luminosity-optimised lattice*.
The ±180 m it simulates **is** the final focus plus the e±-sweeping chicanes; the
0.1–0.6 mrad divergence there is not a design flaw, it is what β\* = 1.5 mm costs.
The paper says so and points at the fix:

> "the current design is optimized for the collider luminosity and not for
> neutrino physics, so dedicated straight sections for neutrino and muon beam
> dump physics may be required."

For this study that is the whole ballgame, because our 700 m insertion is a
*proposal*, not the v0.9 lattice. A dedicated dispersion-free drift of half-length
ℓ, matched with a waist β\* = ℓ, has

  σ<sub>θ</sub> = √(ε/ℓ) = **1.2 µrad** for ℓ = 350 m, with σ<sub>x</sub> ≤ 0.6 mm

— i.e. a *true* 1/γ pencil at a trivially small aperture. So the corridor's pencil
is achievable; it just cannot be the final focus, and it has to be length *added*
beyond the ±180 m the FF and chicanes consume. (The paper's own estimate — "β to
increase by ~100×, aperture 5σ from 5 cm to 50 cm" — is a heuristic scaling of
the *existing* high-α straight; a purpose-built waisted drift is far cheaper in
aperture. Worth raising with the authors.)

What the corridor's numbers look like as a function of how much dedicated
low-divergence straight is built, with the remainder at 0.15 mrad:

| dedicated pencil length | on-axis flux density vs the published value | reading |
|---|---|---|
| 688 m of 700 m | 0.98× | what the site claims today — requires the FF and chicanes to be collinear, which they cannot be |
| 340 m (700 m − IMCC's ±180 m) | **0.49×** | the realistic ceiling: everything not FF/chicane made a pencil |
| 150 m | 0.22× | matches `L_PENCIL`; a modest dedicated section |
| 0 m | 0.010× | the current IMCC lattice — the paper's result |

So the honest range is a factor **2–100** below the published flux densities,
depending on a machine-design decision this study has not yet made explicit.
Making that decision explicit is the single highest-value revision available:

1. State that the corridor concept **requires a dedicated low-divergence
   neutrino straight** downstream of the final focus and chicanes, quote
   σ<sub>θ</sub> = √(ε/ℓ) for it, and give the lattice-design ask (a
   dispersion-free waisted drift, β\* ≈ ℓ, sub-mm beam).
2. Publish the two-component beam — pencil core (weight *f*) plus 0.15 mrad
   final-focus/chicane halo (weight 1−*f*) plus the 3 mrad chicane plateau — with
   *f* as the study's new headline parameter, replacing `L_PENCIL`.
3. Note the **tension this creates**: *f* → 1 maximises detector rate and
   minimises exit-strip footprint, but re-sharpens the plume and pushes the
   exit-strip dose back up toward the raw King values. The current site presents
   a sharp pencil for the physics case and a smeared one for the safety case;
   with the paper's numbers those can no longer be assumed independently.

## 8. Assessment

The paper is bad news for one page and good news for the study. The siting
argument — which is what this study is actually *for* — does not depend on the
beam being 2.7 cm wide; it depends on where the centreline goes, and that is
untouched. The safety and aquifer cases, which are the arguments that would
actually be contested in a siting review, both come out **stronger**, and the
single fudge factor the safety page flagged as dominant is now pinned by a real
lattice.

What breaks is the detector story: the beam is not smaller than a truck-portable
target, the per-tonne rates are ~10² too high, and the prism effect — attractive
because it promised energy selection by radius for free — is not there. The
ν<sub>τ</sub>-appearance section keeps its structure and loses two orders of
magnitude in rate, which moves it from "two orders of magnitude beyond the world
sample" to "competitive with it, if τ-ID improves".

And the study now has a machine-design question it was implicitly answering
without saying so: *is the corridor beam radiated by the final focus, or by a
dedicated low-divergence straight built beyond it?* The published numbers assume
the second and cite parameters from the first. Making that choice explicit, with
*f* as a stated parameter and the safety/physics tension it creates, is the
revision that would most improve the study.

*Numbers in this note come from the paper's own figures and quoted values, plus
scalings computed against `tools/corridor_layout.py` at σ<sub>θ</sub> = 0.15 mrad.
The ν<sub>τ</sub> background estimates in §5.2 are scalings, not simulations.*

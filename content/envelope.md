A neutrino beam cannot be fenced. Between its two emergence points it passes
over or under thousands of parcels of land that the laboratory will never
own. This note sets out the legal geometry that the siting baseline is built
around, and then walks the whole 200+ km line, segment by segment, against
it. The numbers are the [interactive tool](../tool/)'s baseline (IP at
41.8443° N, 191 m ASL, 15.40 mrad), whose civic-envelope audit table
recomputes this walk live for any configuration.

**The rule:** wherever either beam is within **500 ft (152 m) of the ground
surface — above it or below it — the surface should belong to an
institutional partner**: the Department of Energy, the ComEd transmission
easement the corridor follows, or the University of Illinois. Above 500 ft
the beam is in public navigable airspace; 500 ft down it is below every
private well, basement, and utility on the route. Private land is crossed
only where the beam is too high or too deep for any surface owner's use of
their property to be touched.

## 1. Upward: who owns the sky

* The old common-law maxim *cuius est solum, eius est usque ad coelum* ("who
  owns the soil owns up to the heavens") has not been federal law since
  ***United States v. Causby*, 328 U.S. 256 (1946)**: a landowner owns only
  the airspace they can *reasonably use and occupy* — the immediate reaches
  above the land — and the airspace above that is a **public highway**.
* The floor of that public highway is operationally defined by
  **14 CFR 91.119**: minimum safe altitudes of **500 ft over uncongested
  land** and **1,000 ft over congested areas**. Below those altitudes,
  Causby-style takings claims are possible; above them, they are not (absent
  actual interference).
* This study therefore treats **+500 ft AGL as the top of the civic
  envelope** — with the 1,000 ft congested-area floor checked separately
  where the beam is still climbing (it passes 1,000 ft AGL near 42.03° N,
  over low-density Wayne countryside, before reaching any congested
  suburb).

A neutrino beam, unlike an aircraft, makes no noise, casts no shadow, and
deposits nothing measurable in the air it crosses (open-air showers need
hundreds of metres of *dense* material to develop; the dose case for
overflight is bounded in the [safety assessment](../safety/)). The 500 ft
rule is thus a *stronger* standard than the physics requires — it is chosen
so that the argument never has to leave settled airspace law.

## 2. Downward: who owns the deep

* Subsurface ownership has no federal bright line; Illinois follows the
  modern trend that the *ad coelum* maxim's downward branch, too, reaches
  only as deep as the owner's **reasonable use**. The practical envelope of
  reasonable use along this corridor:
  * **private wells** in the Silurian dolomite aquifer: typically 50–120 m;
  * **foundations, geothermal loops, utilities**: < 150 m everywhere;
  * **Chicago's TARP ("Deep Tunnel")** runs 45–110 m beneath thousands of
    private parcels under recorded subsurface easements acquired at nominal
    cost — the regional precedent that deep crossings are routine to
    permit.
* This study takes **−500 ft as the floor of the civic envelope** —
  three-plus times deeper than any private well on the route — and notes
  that unlike TARP, *nothing is built* under anyone's land: no tunnel, no
  vibration, no subsidence risk, no physical entry at all. The only thing
  that crosses is a flux of neutrinos of which roughly one in 10¹¹ interacts
  over the whole 198 km chord (the groundwater consequences are audited to
  be orders of magnitude below drinking-water limits in the
  [aquifer note](../aquifer/)).

## 4. The honest exceptions, and why they hold up

The rule as stated has four ⚠ rows — stretches inside the ±500 ft envelope
whose surface is private. None involves the beam touching the surface;
each sits in the gap between "what airspace/depth law clearly protects" and
"what the physics actually requires" — and each has an independent
resolution:

1. **N4 — 1.9 km at 384–500 ft over Wayne.** Above the rooftops by more
   than a football field, above helicopter minimums, no noise, no light, no
   measurable dose (the beam is a metre-scale pencil hundreds of feet up).
   Causby protects the "immediate reaches" an owner can use; a silent,
   invisible flux 380+ ft up does not plausibly reach that standard — and
   if a future court disagreed, *avigation easements* (the standard airport
   instrument for exactly this altitude band) over ~a dozen parcels close
   the question.
2. **S2 — 5.6 km at 74–152 m under Aurora's east edge.** Deeper than every
   well in the neighbourhood (municipal Aurora pumps from far deeper
   sandstones, crossed 22+ km out where the audit gives 120× margin under
   the drinking-water limit); nothing physical present. TARP-style recorded
   subsurface easements are available at nominal cost if wanted — but with
   no structure and no measurable effect, there is no interference to
   compensate.
3. **S3/S4 — the Urbana passage, 66–119 m under the city and campus.** Same
   class as S2, with the university itself as the principal surface owner
   near the south end; the [aquifer audit](../aquifer/) puts the stagnant
   worst-case groundwater activation here at four orders of magnitude below
   the EPA limit.
4. **The 300-vs-500 ft band at Smith Road** (the tool's red band) is
   *cleared entirely* in the baseline: the beam crosses the nearest
   rooftops 380 ft up, above even the conservative private-airspace
   ceiling the tool lets you set.

The envelope argument, in one sentence: **the beam meets the ground only on
partner land, is otherwise never within 500 ft of private surface except
where it is legally overhead airspace or legally inert deep crossing, and
both of those residual bands come with standard, inexpensive real-estate
instruments (avigation and subsurface easements) if belt-and-suspenders
formality is wanted.**

## 5. What this replaces

The deep reference layout (design note §2) accepts two *unchosen*
surface-grazing exit strips ~30–40 km out and manages them with easements
after the fact. The civic-envelope configuration inverts that: it spends
15.40 mrad of ring tilt (one LEP of plane inclination) to *choose* both
exits — one inside the fence, one on a partner university's farm — so that
the study's land-use story can be told entirely in this note's vocabulary:
public airspace above 500 ft, inert transit below 500 ft, and grounded
beam only on institutional land.

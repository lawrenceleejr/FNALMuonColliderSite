Every other page of this study integrates over a year. This one takes a
single 0.2 s machine cycle apart, because the corridor is not one beam — it
is **five beams sharing one axis**, separated cleanly in time. The RCS
straights are co-aligned on the meridian and their ring planes are pitched
(4.32 / 2.16 mrad down-to-north) precisely so that their plumes **converge
on the same deep detector hall** as the collider plume (9.25 km from the IP,
107 m below grade — the deep reference layout of the
[design note](../design/) §2). At 5 Hz, what that hall sees each cycle is a
7 ms **energy chirp** climbing from 63 GeV to 5 TeV as the chain ramps,
followed by 193 ms of 5 TeV store. Everything on this page is generated by
[`tools/chain_timing.py`](https://github.com/lawrenceleejr/FNALMuonColliderSite/blob/main/tools/chain_timing.py)
(machine-readable: [chain_timing.json](../geo/chain_timing.json)).

**Three sites, one clock.** This page tracks the beam at the corridor's
three detector locations: the **near hall** (1.3 km, 15 m deep — the
civic-envelope baseline's on-site detector), the **on-site convergence
hall** (9.25 km, 107 m deep — the deep reference layout's hall, where the
RCS pitches converge the whole chain), and the **UIUC far hall**
(197.4 km — the South Farms exit of the *south* beam, so its flavours are
the CP mirror, ν̄μ + νe). The far hall is collider-only *in the baseline*: the south-going
RCS plumes, pitched down-to-north, leave the ground 11.5 and 21 km south
and pass 3.4–3.9 km *above* the surface at the exit (the
[co-tilted chain variant](../uiuc-chain/) re-pitches every ring to put the
chirp on the far hall too). Its pulses arrive a
fixed 658 µs of flight later — an offset, not a structure.

**Headline:** the acceleration chain is not a sideshow. Because the RCS
straights are FODO straights with no final focus, their plumes are **true
1/γ pencils** — the corridor's only kinematic pencils, now that
[arXiv:2608.02718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02718) has shown the collider
plume is divergence-smeared (see the [beam-size study](../beamsize/)).
Converged on the hall, the chain delivers **71 % of the collider store's
on-axis fluence and 39 % of its interaction rate** — in 3.5 % of the cycle,
inside its own time gates, with the energy of every pulse known from the
turn number.

## 1. Anatomy of a cycle

One bunch per sign is injected into RCS1 at 63 GeV every 0.2 s. Ramp
durations below are solved from the per-stage decay fractions the
[safety assessment](../safety/) already assumes (9/9/7/7 %) — they land in
the same 0.3–6 ms class as the IMCC interim-report chain. The bunch passes
the aligned straight once per turn per sign; each pass puts a ~5 ps pulse of
neutrinos on the hall (one sign northward, the other southward).

| stage | E (GeV) | ramp | turns | pulse spacing | decays aimed north / cycle | survival |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RCS1 (C = 6.28 km) | 63 → 314 | 0.29 ms | 14 | 20.96 µs | 1.24 × 10¹⁰ | 0.91 |
| RCS2 (same tunnel) | 314 → 750 | 0.94 ms | 45 | 20.96 µs | 1.13 × 10¹⁰ | 0.91 |
| RCS3 (C = 14.72 km) | 750 → 1500 | 1.58 ms | 32 | 49.11 µs | 3.10 × 10⁹ | 0.93 |
| RCS4 (same tunnel) | 1500 → 5000 | 4.23 ms | 86 | 49.11 µs | 2.89 × 10⁹ | 0.93 |
| collider (C = 11.0 km) | 5000 | 193 ms store | 5260 | 36.69 µs | 7.01 × 10¹⁰ | 0.16 |

Totals per cycle, per sign: the chain contributes 3.0 × 10¹⁰ decays aimed
north against the store's 7.0 × 10¹⁰ — the chirp carries **30 % of the
corridor's neutrinos by count**. Two bookkeeping notes, stated plainly:
the 9/9/7/7 % decay fractions imply a chain transmission of 0.72, less
generous than the 0.90 used to normalise the collider numbers elsewhere in
this study (the collider rows here inherit the 0.90 for consistency with
those pages); and only 84 % of stored muons decay before the next injection
— the store factor now applied throughout the study.

order 10⁻⁷.

**Across cycles** the picture interleaves: each bunch stores for the full
0.2 s (dumped 85 % decayed when its successor arrives), and the *next*
bunch's 7 ms chirp runs concurrently through the last 7 ms of the store.
Wall-clock, the hall sees a sawtooth store with a chirp column inside each
overlap window; the mean-energy profile dives from 1.67 TeV to ~250 GeV
during the overlap — the chirp's instantaneous rate is ~15× the store's —
and snaps back at each injection. (The single-cycle figures on this page
follow one bunch, chain-first; same physics, bunch-centric phase.)

## 2. Time gating is essentially perfect

Three pulse trains interleave: 20.96 µs (RCS1/2 tunnel), 49.11 µs (RCS3/4),
36.69 µs (collider). Because each pulse is picoseconds wide and the machine
clock is known, **every neutrino event time-stamps its parent ring
unambiguously** — the probability that an RCS pulse lands within even 1 ns
of a store pulse is ~10⁻⁴ per pulse, and a real detector gates far tighter
than that. The chirp does not contaminate the 5 TeV physics sample, and the
5 TeV store does not contaminate the chirp: they are separate experiments
sharing a hall. (Within one ramp the turn number tags the energy: the
chirp is a sequence of ~180 quasi-monochromatic-parent pulses per cycle,
63 GeV → 5 TeV, repeated 5 times a second.) Time-of-flight offsets between
the stacked straights are sub-µs constants and drop out.

The one caveat is not timing but **steering policy**: the safety case wobbles
the RCS straights (±1 mrad movers) to dilute their own far exit strips
(Zone C of the [safety assessment](../safety/)). Wobbled, the RCS pencils
sweep a ±9 m band across the hall instead of sitting on axis — the chirp's
per-pulse brightness at a fixed small detector drops by roughly the ratio of
band to pencil width (×10–10³ depending on energy). Chirp physics therefore
wants the movers **programmed, not random**: the same mover system that
dilutes the exit strip can step the pencil across the hall deterministically,
so a detector array (or a movable target) keeps the pencil while the
year-averaged exit strip still sees a diluted band. That is a control-room
choice, not a hardware change — but it must be chosen.

* **The RCS bands keep the prism.** A pencil beam's energy falls off axis as

* **The collider blob does not.** Its 0.15 mrad divergence smears the core

## 4. Flavor vs time — and flavor evolution

The north-aimed flux is one muon sign: **exactly one νμ and one ν̄e per
decay** (CP mirror selectable by circulation direction), at every instant of
the cycle. Because σν ∝ E for both species, the *interacting* flavor shares
barely move either: cycle-averaged **48 % νμ CC : 24 % ν̄e CC : 29 % NC** at
the convergence hall, time-invariant to a couple of per cent through the whole
chirp. The per-flavor interaction rates carry the timing structure instead —
and the near hall's version makes the two-hall split visible: it is silent
for the first 7 ms of every (bunch-centric) cycle.

<div style="margin:1.2rem 0"><img src="../figs/chain_flavor_near.svg" alt="Per-flavor interaction rates vs time at the near hall" style="max-width:100%"></div>

(The far hall's shares are the CP mirror — ν̄μ CC : νe CC : NC at
44 : 27 : 29 — flat through its store-only cycle.) The one genuinely
time-dependent flavor is the one the beam is born without: **ντ from
oscillation**. The appearance probability scales as
(L/E)², so as the chirp sweeps the parent energy down to tens of GeV the
oscillated fraction of the arriving fluence swings **~560×** through the
cycle at the convergence hall — from a 1.1 × 10⁻⁹ minimum at the RCS4 chirp end,
through 1.4 × 10⁻⁸ in the store, to 6.5 × 10⁻⁷ (47× the store) during the
RCS1 turns. Two subtleties the figure encodes: the RCS4 chirp end dips
*below* the store (same parent energy, but a pencil's on-axis prism spectrum
is harder than the smeared store's, and ⟨P⟩ is dominated by the soft tail);
and the near hall sits ~15× lower throughout, its (1.3/9.25)² baseline
penalty partly offset by the same tail. Absolute rates stay tiny — this is
the corridor's oscillation *window*, not yet an experiment — but the
time-tagged (L/E)-scan structure is unique to a chirped beam.

<div style="margin:1.2rem 0"><img src="../figs/chain_flavor_osc.svg" alt="Mean oscillation probability vs time in the cycle at the three sites" style="max-width:100%"></div>

At the **UIUC far hall** the oscillated fraction is no longer a curiosity:
⟨P(ν̄μ→ν̄τ)⟩ ≈ **5.2 × 10⁻⁶** through the store — 380× the convergence
hall — and turning probability into *counts* produces the corridor's
tidiest plot. The **number of ντ arriving per unit time and area** is
flux × P, and since flux falls as 1/L² while P grows as L², **all three
sites receive essentially the same oscillated-ντ areal density**:
0.011 / 0.008 / 0.006 ντ per cm² per cycle at 9.25 / 1.3 / 197.4 km — a
baseline-independence the [beam-size study](../beamsize/) §5 states as a
per-tonne rate, here visible live in the time domain (the residual spread
is the soft-tail acceptance of each site's core). The chirp breaks the
tie: its (L/E)² blow-up at low energy delivers **30 % of the convergence
hall's oscillated ντ in 3.5 % of the cycle**, towering three decades over
the store in arrival rate. (Oscillation only — the rock-produced ντ
background of [beam-size §5](../beamsize/) is a separate, unmodelled
population here; the far hall remains where oscillated ντ dominate.)

## 5. What the chirp is for

A turn-tagged 63 GeV → 5 TeV sweep, five times a second, in a flavour-pure
beam with known parent kinematics, is an instrument in its own right:

1. **A cross-section programme per cycle.** σν(E) from 100 GeV to 5 TeV in
   one apparatus with shared systematics — the νμ/ν̄e (or CP-mirror) blend
   at every point of the DIS-to-propagator transition, at rates of 10⁵–10⁸
   events per tonne-year per stage. The chirp turns the corridor's fixed
   5 TeV line into an energy scan.
2. **Energy calibration for free.** The turn number predicts each pulse's
   parent energy to the ramp model's accuracy; the detector's energy scale
   can be tracked against it continuously, cycle by cycle.
3. **A flux anchor.** The RCS pencils are kinematic — their profile is pure
   decay physics, no lattice divergence to model — so the chirp bands give
   the flux normalisation a handle the smeared 5 TeV component lacks.

## 6. Caveats

* Linear-in-time ramps, no injection/extraction gaps, no transfer-line
  decays; bunch-to-bunch transfer assumed instantaneous. Real ramp shapes
  redistribute the chirp within its 7 ms without changing totals.
* RCS optics are represented by a 20 µrad divergence bound (FODO-class,
  ≪ 1/γ at every chain energy); a real RCS lattice adds structure at that
  scale, not at the 1/γ scale that sets the bands.
* The deep-hall convergence uses the deep reference layout (collider at
  100 m, pitch 0). The civic-envelope split of the two halls is described
  in §3; re-solving the RCS pitches onto the near hall would need ~35–50
  mrad ring-plane tilts and is not credible.
* Decay fractions (hence ramp times) are the dose model's bounding
  assumptions; the resulting 0.72 chain transmission vs the 0.90 used for
  collider normalisation is an inherited inconsistency of this study, now
  flagged in both places. Wobble policy per §2.
